## What we build

Saga Education runs high-impact tutoring in public schools, and builds the
software that makes it work. The products are tools for running tutoring
programs end to end: scheduling periods and pods, staffing tutors and
assigning students, keeping rosters current, taking attendance, assessing
progress, surveying students, and reporting on all of it to the people
accountable for the program. Most of that lives in the dashboard, a
form-heavy, table-heavy tool used all day on a laptop.

Two products have their own use cases. **Saga Connect** hosts the tutoring
session itself: audio and video, a shared whiteboard, individual
scratchpads, chat, an expression editor and calculator for math, area
models, algebra tiles and Desmos graphing. It makes working with students
easy whether in person or online, and it is used on laptops and tablets,
often in a noisy classroom. **Saga Coach** is a self-paced training portal
that turns non-specialists into effective tutors; it is built around video,
practice and rubrics, and it is free to anyone who tutors or mentors.

Around both sits a growing set of coaching tools that improve the efficacy
of the tutorials: observation and the QTF skills rubric, session recordings
with AI analysis, and feedback that gives tutors practical strategies and
lets program leads track quality over time.

## Who uses it

- **District and program administrators** configure programs, read reports,
  and answer to districts. They want the numbers, the exceptions, and a
  clean export. They visit in bursts and expect to find the same thing in
  the same place each time.
- **Site leads and coordinators** run the day: periods, pods, attendance,
  roster changes, the student who moved sections. They work fast between
  classes and value dense tables, keyboard-friendly forms and obvious undo.
- **Tutors** are often early in their careers and new to the tools. They
  live in Connect during sessions and in Coach between them. Their screens
  need to be calm, large-target and forgiving, with nothing that pulls
  attention away from the student.
- **Students** see Connect and the occasional survey. Everything they touch
  is simple, legible and quick, and never reads as a test of the software.
- **Coaches and program quality staff** watch sessions, score rubrics and
  give feedback. They need video, timelines and annotations side by side,
  and comparisons across tutors and weeks.

## What that asks of a page

Lead with the summary and put the detail one click away. Prefer a table to
a card wall when the user is scanning many of the same thing. Make the
primary action obvious and keep destructive ones apart. Write in the
vocabulary of a tutoring program (program, period, pod, tutor, student,
session, day type, roster, district, site) and never in the vocabulary of
the system. Assume a laptop first, a tablet for Connect, and a phone only
for quick checks.
